An agenda is not paperwork. It is the meeting's operating plan.

A good agenda is not a list of topics. It is a plan for getting from where the meeting starts to the outcome you need.

For customer-facing meetings: a useful agenda keeps the conversation anchored in customer priorities, questions, decisions, and next steps instead of becoming a product dump.

For internal sales meetings: a useful agenda keeps pipeline, forecast, account, and deal conversations focused on decisions and actions rather than status updates alone.

Six parts. Practice each one.

Purpose → Outcome → Topics → Owner → Time → Next Step. Each mini-challenge asks you to make one meeting decision and then explains the tradeoff.

02 • Build it

Your meeting

Use the same six-part framework. The builder will coach you as you go.

Start with the meeting

Choose a starter. Customer-facing starters emphasize customer priorities and next steps; internal starters emphasize decisions and actions. Everything remains editable after you choose.
1 • Purpose

Why does this meeting need to happen?

Purpose is the reason this meeting deserves time. For customer-facing meetings, anchor it in customer priorities; for internal meetings, anchor it in the business decision or alignment needed.
2 • Outcome

What should be true by the end?

Outcome describes what should be decided, understood, clarified, identified, or assigned.

Add up to five concrete outcomes.

3 • Topics

What needs to happen to reach the outcome?

Topics work best when written as actions. “Pricing” is a subject; “Decide whether the pricing structure fits the rollout plan” tells the group what to do.
4 + 5 • Ownership & Time

Give every important part an owner.

Owner says who leads the item. Time says how much attention it deserves. These fields are built into every agenda item above.
6 • Next Step

Leave time to close the loop.

Next Step is where the meeting converts conversation into a decision, action, owner, or checkpoint.
